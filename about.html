About DSP Records

DSP Records began as an underground event and artist management company and has evolved into a record label built on structure, growth, and real connection.

Origins — Dyrtie Sound Productions

DSP began in the early 2010s as Dyrtie Sound Productions, a small but structured underground promotion and artist management company.

It started with monthly shows at Whiskey Creek in Medicine Hat, Alberta — a bar with a dedicated live music room attached to the main space. The venue wasn’t designed specifically for bass-driven electronic events, so DSP approached it differently.

We brought in our own sound.

We brought in our own lighting.

Not to inflate the scale — but to build the environment properly.

The sound systems were configured specifically for bass-heavy electronic music, tuned for clarity and weight rather than background volume. Lighting was added to shape the room intentionally — giving each event its own atmosphere instead of relying on a default bar setup.

Load-in, setup, calibration, teardown — everything was handled directly. These nights were built piece by piece.

Strong sound.
Focused lighting.
Curated lineups.
A crowd that showed up for the music.

None of it was massive.
But it was deliberate.
And it built momentum.

Growth & Weekly Momentum

As consistency built trust, DSP began booking touring headliners who were appearing on major festival lineups at the time. These artists were paired with strong local support, gradually raising the level of production while maintaining the underground foundation that made the nights feel authentic.

From monthly events, DSP moved into structured weekly Saturday programming. That shift created real opportunity — not just for the core DSP roster, but for a broader circle of local DJs who were ready for consistent stage time and needed real exposure.

The intention was never exclusivity. It was momentum through inclusion.

As production standards rose, the scale of what DSP was building eventually outgrew the original space. The direction moved forward naturally.

Artist Management & Operational Discipline

DSP was not simply a promotion brand.

It operated as a legitimate artist management company, with formal agreements and structured booking efforts. At its height, DSP had five DJs signed under management, working to secure bookings outside their hometowns, support early festival appearances, coordinate travel logistics, and help build professional credibility.

The structure was real. The expectations were clear.

One of the defining operational milestones was Halloween 2012.

Over the course of two days, DSP DJs performed three shows across two different cities. Every time slot was scheduled. Every transition was coordinated. Every show ran smoothly.

No logistical breakdowns.
No missed communication.
No unnecessary friction.

That level of coordination — across cities, venues, and artists — became the foundation for future projects.

DSP’s final nightclub production was New Year’s Eve 2013 (December 31, 2012). Shortly after, due to life obligations and shifting priorities, DSP stepped away from promotions and artist management.

There was no collapse.
No fallout.
Just real life.

The brand went quiet — but it never disappeared.

UK Radio Takeover — Staying Connected

During the COVID lockdown years, when dancefloors were closed worldwide, DSP organized a Canadian takeover of a UK-based radio station.

On the last Sunday of each month, DSP coordinated up to twelve DJs from across Canada to broadcast live into the UK airwaves. Time slots were scheduled, communication was managed across provinces and time zones, and the underground stayed connected during a time when live events were not possible.

It wasn’t about exposure.
It was about continuity.

The systems built during the event years — coordination, communication, structure — were still intact.

They simply adapted to a new platform.

Culture & Core Values

DSP was built inside nightlife environments — spaces that can sometimes carry ego, politics, or unnecessary division. Yet every DSP event maintained a reputation for people showing up to enjoy the music and respect each other.

That culture was intentional.

DSP is rooted in the principles of Peace. Love. Unity. Respect.

Not as decoration.
As practice.

PLUR is not meant to disappear when the sun comes up. It is meant to extend into how artists communicate, collaborate, share stages, and build careers. The energy that brings people together on a dancefloor should carry into contracts, bookings, and long-term relationships.

DSP’s values are not theoretical.

They were not adopted because they look good on a website.

They were developed over time — through lived experience, through growth, and through choosing to move differently.

The standards upheld today are the result of years of reflection and rebuilding.
Not perfection. Progress.

Growth is part of the culture.

Artists should lift each other up.
Politics should not outweigh contribution.
Integrity should guide decisions.
Momentum should be built, not manufactured.

Community is not branding. It is responsibility.

From Events to Record Label

After years of live event production and artist management, DSP shifted its focus toward recorded music and structured digital releases.

DSP Records is the evolution of that foundation.

The same discipline that went into sound systems, lighting rigs, weekly scheduling, and multi-city coordination now goes into:

• Structured release planning
• Cohesive visual branding
• Professional presentation
• Long-term artist development
• Consistent momentum

The scale may change.
The values do not.

DSP Records exists as a platform built on real-world experience — not theory.

Where DSP Is Now

DSP was publicly inactive for many years due to life obligations. It did not operate continuously for decades. If it had, there would be far more to display.

But what was built during those early years was solid.

The brand never burned out.
It stayed smoldering.

Now it returns with clearer direction and stronger systems — not simply to recreate what once was, but to build forward with structure, discipline, and community at the center.

There is no need to exaggerate the past.

The foundation is real.
The experience is earned.
The direction is steady.

Bigger stages may come.
Expanded collaborations may grow.
The dancefloor will return.

Because raves don’t end at night.
They end when the sun comes up.

And what matters is what you carry with you afterward.

Built systems.
Long-term momentum.
Real connection.

Always fresh. Still dirty.
